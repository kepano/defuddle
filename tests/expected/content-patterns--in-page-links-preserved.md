Over three weeks we walked the upper river and recorded the width, depth, and flow at twelve stations.[^1] The results are summarized in [Table 1](#table-1) and discussed in the sections below.

## Method

At each station we stretched a tape across the channel and measured depth every half meter. Flow was timed with a float over a ten meter reach, repeated three times and averaged.[^2]

- [Station A](#station-a) sits below the old mill, where the banks are steep and lined with stone walls.
- [Station B](#station-b) is on a wide gravel bar that floods every spring and shifts after each storm.
- [Station C](#station-c) marks the confluence with the northern tributary and the start of the lower reach.

| Station | Width (m) | Depth (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 6.2 | 0.8 |
| B | 11.5 | 0.4 |

## Results

The channel widened steadily downstream while average depth fell, which matches what we expected from the gravel deposits. The next survey will repeat the same stations in autumn to compare seasonal flow.
